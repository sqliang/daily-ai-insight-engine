The hosted page records anonymous usage — which controls get used and how far they’re turned, and which patch settings get exported — to improve the rack. Aggregated only, no accounts, no personal data, nothing typed is ever read. A local copy sends nothing.

It’s a little machine that builds techno loops by itself. Press the button and it starts playing.

Don’t like what you hear? Hit **Reroll** for a whole new one. Turn any knob to change the sound. That’s the whole thing — just play.

Click a chain slot to cycle it through the scenes. With Chain on, the rack walks the slots and swaps scenes on the bar — patterns cut, knobs morph.

Filter is bipolar — centre is off, left low‑passes, right high‑passes. Pads are momentary; hold them.

Kick fed into the long chamber, low‑passed and ducked. The floor of the whole track — bounce it as its own stem.

Continuous detuned pair, slow filter.

Resonant noise band. The room tone and the risers.

Sing, talk, or feed a line in — it lands where every module lands:
ducked by the kick, chopped by the clock, into the same plate and delay.
**Wear headphones.** Throw pushes the delay send and its feedback while held;
Cut kills the dry so only the tail carries. Shift adds a pitched layer under
your voice — left for the octave‑down monster, right for the sped‑up hook.
A live take (Capture) records it; an offline bounce cannot, because there is no
microphone inside an offline render.

Grains are cut from the rack’s own output a moment ago, then thrown back in pitched and scattered. Send opens the feed; Spray decides how far back they reach. It renders offline too, so a bounce sprays the same cloud you heard.

A real oscillator wired into the destination’s parameter, running at audio rate — the sidebands are the sound, not a wobble. It tracks the bass note by Ratio, so whole numbers stay harmonic and Fine pulls it into clang.

Published pages can only save a
short list of file types, and audio is not one of them. Everything here
plays and patches normally — to bounce audio, stems or MIDI, open your
local copy of **rack.html**. Patch JSON does save from here.

Bounce renders offline — faster than real time, sample‑exact, no dropouts. Stems come back as one zip, all bar‑aligned so they drop into Ableton on the grid.

Arm, then move any knob while the rack runs. The move is written to a 4‑bar lane, loops from then on, and is baked into every bounce.

MIDI access was declined.

**Space** run/stop · **M** mutate · **R** reroll ·
**F**/**D**/**S** hold for fill/drop/stutter · **1–4** scenes

Knobs: drag, or focus and use arrows (**Shift** = fine).
Steps: click cycles auto → on → off, **Shift**+click sets a ratchet.

Bouncing runs offline, so a heavy patch never drops samples in the exported file even if it stutters live.
