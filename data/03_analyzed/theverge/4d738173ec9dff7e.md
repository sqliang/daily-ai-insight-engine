Confusion is a generally accepted side effect of mystery box shows. They slather on secrets with the promise of a satisfying payoff in the end, and sometimes the cast and crew even have a hard time following what’s going on. But even by the standards of the genre, *Dark Matter* is extreme. Its multiversal premise, which involves a man kidnapping a version of himself from another world, can be hard to wrap your head around, and as the first season went on, things only got harder to follow. With the return of the show for season 2, I have both good and bad news: The show is more dramatic and thrilling than ever, while also being ever so much harder to follow.

# Apple TV’s sci-fi thriller Dark Matter gets even trippier in season 2

First, a reminder of where the story left us. Based on the novel of the same name, *Dark Matter* follows Jason Dessen (Joel Edgerton), a physics professor in Chicago who is living a relatively normal and contented life with his artist wife Daniela (Jennifer Connelly) and teenage son Charlie (Oakes Fegley). In another world, a different version of Jason decided to forgo that family life in pursuit of his dream, which was apparently to build a gigantic box that allowed people to travel to other parallel universes. But that alt version of Jason ultimately wasn’t satisfied with the life he had, and used the box to travel to a world where he and Daniela were together. He then kidnapped himself, abandoned that version in another world, and proceeded to take over his life.

Things did not go as planned for alt Jason. Daniela eventually realized that something was wrong, Jason prime fought his way through multiple worlds to get back home, and by the end of the season, an army of Jason variants had descended on the same version of Chicago in hopes of reuniting with Daniela. At the end of the finale, the original Jason, Daniela, and Charlie had made their way into the box in hopes of finding a new world where they could live out a normal life in peace.

Still with me? Well, good, because things only get more complex from here. *Dark Matter* is obviously a high-concept sci-fi thriller, but in season 2, it really pushes further into its premise, exploring all of the wild possibilities. Jason and his family are living like multiversal nomads, traveling to different worlds in search of a home, and this lets the show get into the nitty-gritty of how the box works and the rules for traveling between worlds. It’s an inexact science: You can’t just go wherever you want. Instead, your destination is dependent on your mindset when you open the door. So if you’re scared or stressed out, get ready for a postapocalyptic version of Chicago.
