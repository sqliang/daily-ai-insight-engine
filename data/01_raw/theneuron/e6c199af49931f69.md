Welcome, humans.

So the datacenter backlash discourse has taken a turn for the worse, with knives coming out on all sides trying to explain away why people hate them so much.

Sam Altman went on [David Senra’s podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kG8AoExkX40&utm_source=www.theneurondaily.com&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=why-sam-altman-thinks-people-hate-ai) and addressed some of the industry’s messaging directly:

[![Image 2](https://media.beehiiv.com/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=auto,onerror=redirect,quality=80/uploads/asset/file/41f608c4-408d-447d-a091-56a8bea219b9/Screenshot_2026-08-23_at_10.49.47_AM.png?t=1787507431)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kG8AoExkX40&utm_source=www.theneurondaily.com&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=why-sam-altman-thinks-people-hate-ai)

He said a lot of AI builders _(subtext for really, mainly Dario)_ have spent years talking about extinction risk and disappearing jobs, then “have not as a field done a very good job” explaining the benefits or how the downsides could be mitigated. His preferred pitch is much more human: AI should give people “more power and personal freedom,” and he thinks it could create “the greatest boom in people starting smaller businesses that we have ever seen.”

The internet immediately stress-tested that argument. [Andrew Curran](https://x.com/AndrewCurran_/status/2091546008165736722?utm_source=www.theneurondaily.com&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=why-sam-altman-thinks-people-hate-ai) highlighted Altman’s admission, while [Nikola Jurkovic](https://x.com/nikolaj2030/status/2091550683447373868?utm_source=www.theneurondaily.com&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=why-sam-altman-thinks-people-hate-ai) argued the problem isn’t messaging so much as people rejecting the underlying risk-and-trust bargain.
