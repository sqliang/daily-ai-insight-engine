The internet began as a public network anyone could build on. AI is developing more like a row of private theme parks, where a few companies own the rides, write the rules, and keep raising ticket prices.

[Current AI](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/19/nonprofit-current-ai-is-racing-to-build-the-world-wide-web-of-ai-free-for-all/?utm_source=www.theneurondaily.com&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=alibaba-s-2-4t-qwen-joins-the-ai-race) wants to build the public alternative: open AI infrastructure that communities can use, modify, and control for free. The nonprofit has $400M in committed funding and is already backing offline tools for Indigenous communities, datasets covering 50+ African languages, and a pocket-sized device that works in 22 Indian languages without internet access.

The goal is basically a World Wide Web for AI, where progress benefits everyone instead of whichever company owns your login. _Finally, an AI moonshot that does not begin with “please enter a valid credit card.”_

**On a tangent:** We also published a full breakdown of [everything Samsara announced at Beyond 2026](https://www.theneuron.ai/explainer-articles/everything-ai-samsara-announced-at-beyond-2026/?utm_source=www.theneurondaily.com&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=alibaba-s-2-4t-qwen-joins-the-ai-race), including AI agents for physical operations, 360-degree cameras, smarter fleet maintenance, and disposable shipment-tracking labels. The common thread: move AI out of the browser and into the trucks, warehouses, worksites, and supply chains that keep the physical economy moving.
