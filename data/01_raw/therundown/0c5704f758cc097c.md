The Rundown: Anthropic just published research showing that Claude does its thinking in a small, internal “workspace” resembling what neuroscientists credit for human conscious access, with researchers saying the area wasn’t programmed but “emerged on its own.”

The details:

Anthropic found ‘J-space,’ an internal area acting like Claude’s internal notepad, which holds active concepts the model uses and steers while thinking.

J-Space is different from chain-of-thought text, with these internal hints staying off-screen even when they help direct Claude’s next answer.

Editing the unspoken thoughts changes answers: swapping an internal “spider” pattern for “ant” during a question about legs flipped its response from 8 to 6.

When J-space was deleted, Claude still chatted and recalled facts, but multi-step problem completion collapsed, showing where harder thinking happens.

Why it matters: Anthropic has been criticized (most vocally by Microsoft AI head Mustafa Suleyman) for its AI consciousness talk, and while the researchers note this doesn’t reveal “whether Claude is conscious… or feels anything at all,” finding an undesigned, brain-like workspace inside a model is exactly why the lab keeps asking.
