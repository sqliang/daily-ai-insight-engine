Every day, millions of people across Europe use OpenAI’s tools to learn, create, work and manage everyday tasks. Our tools also support businesses of all sizes and governments across the region. We believe responsible AI can help drive Europe’s competitiveness and prosperity. As the EU AI Act enters its next phase, we’re sharing how we have strengthened our approach to safety, security, transparency and provenance in line with the EU framework—and how we will continue to evolve our practices as AI advances.

Our mission is to ensure that artificial general intelligence benefits all of humanity. That mission carries with it an ongoing responsibility to maximize AI’s benefits, broaden access, and manage risks. That is why, for years, we have developed governance approaches and supported workable rules that help people benefit from AI safely, an objective which is central to the EU AI Act. Such rules must be pragmatic, proportionate and risk-based in order to advance governance while supporting innovation. With these principles in mind, we contributed to and endorsed two Codes of Practice: the EU’s General-Purpose AI [GPAI] Code of Practice(opens in a new window) and the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content(opens in a new window), both of which were developed through extensive multi-stakeholder processes and build on work we have advanced across safety, security, transparency, accountability, and provenance.

The GPAI Code creates a shared framework for transparency, safety, and security for general-purpose AI models. Our work to support that framework rests on both internal governance and collaboration with external experts, governments, and peer organizations. For years, we have extensively tested our models prior to releasing them, published system cards(opens in a new window) with major releases, brought outside experts into model testing through our Red Teaming Network, and maintained the public Model Spec(opens in a new window) as a window into how we shape model behavior.

We have also continued to strengthen the governance frameworks behind this work. Our Preparedness Framework, in place since 2023 and updated in 2025, sets out how we identify, evaluate, and manage serious risks from advanced AI systems. Our Frontier Governance Framework builds on that foundation and explains how our safety and security practices align with emerging legal requirements, including the EU AI Act’s GPAI Code. Together, these frameworks help translate responsible AI principles into practical decisions about risk assessment, safeguards, model reporting, security, incident response, external expert input, and ongoing updates.

Responsible AI governance also requires work beyond any one company. Through the Frontier Model Forum, collaborations with US CAISI and UK AISI, and work on third-party evaluation practices, we have supported shared safety research, external testing, and clearer evaluation standards across the ecosystem.
