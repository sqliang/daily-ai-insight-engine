Meta just agreed to make sweeping changes for teens as part of a child safety settlement reached with attorneys general across the US. Under the agreement, Meta must apply new safeguards across Instagram and Facebook that will change how teens interact with the social media platforms, as well as restrict their use during certain times.

# All the ways Instagram and Facebook are changing for teens

The settlement stems from a nationwide lawsuit alleging Meta, Google, TikTok, and Snap failed to protect children and made their platforms “addictive” by design. A group of 51 US states and territories signed the agreement, including New York, New Jersey, California, Nevada, South Carolina, Washington, Illinois, and many more. Most of these protections are required to remain in place for at least 10 years.

The changes are coming to Facebook and Instagram in all US states except for New Mexico, which is in the middle of a legal battle with Meta, and Florida, which has banned kids under 14 from creating social media accounts. Texas also reached a separate settlement with Meta with similar requirements. Here’s a list of the biggest changes:

**A non-personalized feed option**: In four months, Facebook and Instagram must give teens the “reasonably accessible” option of a default feed that isn’t personalized by Meta’s recommendation engine. Meta will occasionally send teens reminders about this option, and parents will also be able to set it as their child’s default setting.**Age assurance:**Meta has agreed to ramp up efforts to identify and apply restrictions to underage users on the platform. Under the settlement, Meta must either continue to develop its own age assurance standard or adopt a third-party option. The method must evaluate whether a user is under 13, so they can be removed from the platform, or identify if someone is aged 13 to 17, in which case they would receive teen account protections. New users who haven’t yet been evaluated by Meta’s system will receive teen protections by default.
