As *The Verge*’s resident handheld reviewer, I have nearly every portable gaming PC on a shelf in my house. The MSI Claw 8 EX AI Plus is now the first one I reach for. Thanks to a next-gen Intel chip and improved MSI design, the Claw EX is the most powerful handheld that truly feels portable. It’s the handheld with the longest battery life, and the first Windows handheld I can trust to sleep and wake reliably.

# The MSI Claw EX is the most important PC handheld since Steam Deck — I still wouldn’t buy one

Intel and MSI’s handheld is a preview of a potato-free portable future.

Like the Steam Deck, when I press the power button, my game is always right where I left off. Unlike the Steam Deck, it can hibernate for days without losing a significant amount of charge.

Performance *and* battery? Windows *and* sleep? I wish I could say “you can have your cake and eat it too.” But you can’t. You probably shouldn’t.

You can’t because this is a Ferris Bueller Product Review — this handheld costs a staggering $1,800, and you’ll be lucky to find it even at that price. (MSI’s Anne Lee tells me the US should have limited supply sometime this month.)

Even if you could, you probably shouldn’t because Intel and MSI and Microsoft didn’t fully stick the landing. This is indeed a milestone for the handheld gaming space. But like actual milestones on a highway, you should probably drive right past.

#### The Good

- This Windows handheld sleeps reliably
- A leap forward in handheld performance
- Can trade that performance for good battery life

#### The Bad

- Graphical and performance issues in some games
- $1,800 for a handheld
- Many Windows annoyances remain

The Claw EX does have comfortable grips, I’ll give it that. Those manta-ray-wings wonderfully spread out its weight, to the point that the 1.73-pound (785g) portable feels far more comfortable than the 1.58-pound (715g) Xbox Ally X when I hold it above me in bed.

And while a $1,800 handheld should really have an amazing OLED screen, I definitely prefer the Claw EX’s 8-inch 1920 x 1200 48-120Hz VRR screen to the comparatively cramped 7-inch 1080p one in the Allys. The only real problem is glare — the ultra-glossy screen was *not* great on my road trip this summer.

But the controls are merely average, the fan is loud (though it’s *whoosh* not *whine)*, and those manta-ray grips can buzz my hands when I turn up the speakers. Sound reverberates inside.

No, the Claw EX’s true strengths are performance and battery — but even there, the handheld sometimes let me down.

I’ve always loved the phrase “potato graphics.” It’s such a fun way to describe what games look like when your rig is too weak. They’re rough and pasty — like a potato! — even when running smoothly. Or if you turn up the graphics anyway, you get a “slideshow” because your hardware can’t deliver enough frames.
