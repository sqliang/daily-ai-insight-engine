# 😺 The Pope’s Warning on AI's Babel

## PLUS: US Gov wants to stop “violent anti-tech extremism”

Welcome, humans.

Remember our livestream last week with Ben Cherry from LiveKit? We turned it into a full guide on how to build real-time AI voice agents that can listen, respond, interrupt, call tools, and work in production.

The jump from chatbot to voice agent is bigger than it sounds. A chatbot waits for you to type. A voice agent has to handle timing, interruptions, latency, and the very human habit of changing your mind mid-sentence. *It has to survive Thanksgiving dinner with API access.*

**Here’s what happened in AI today: **

😿 AI backlash became a fight over data centers.

📰 OpenRouter raised $113M as model routing exploded.

📰 Researchers said language models may need sleep.

🍪 Alexa can now generate custom podcasts.

🎓 Map who gets AI's upside before pitching change.


# 😿 **AI's backlash has a physical address now**

Data centers have made AI a local issue: they involve local land, power, water, tax deals, zoning, and neighborhoods living near the hum.

**Now, two institutions are finally reacting:**** **the U.S. government is preparing for anti-tech unrest, while the Catholic Church warns against techno-corporate Babel.

**The State’s response:**

WIRED obtained 1,000+ unpublished DHS, FBI, and fusion-center pages showing law enforcement tracking “anti-technology extremists” amid CEO attacks, data center protests, and job fears.

A New York report warned AI could fuel “large-scale protests” and “anti-tech violent extremist activity,” a phrase absent from existing public DHS or FBI reports.

US President Trump planned to sign an AI Executive Order last week that would request AI companies share their models with the gov ahead up to 90 days in advance of release,, but it got scrapped at the last minute; read the full draft here.


That elasticity is the risk. Anti-tech violence is very real (*just *look at what happened to Sam Altman*)*, but critics of the term worry the label could widen to peaceful protesters, skeptics, and local residents who don’t want a datacenter in their backyard. WIRED even flagged a nonviolent More Perfect Union data center video as a threat vector.

**The Church’s response:** In Magnifica Humanitas, Pope Leo XIV frames AI as “Babel” versus “Jerusalem.” Babel is uniformity, domination, self-sufficiency, and efficiency that sacrifices human dignity. Jerusalem is a shared rebuilding, where everyone contributes.

The Pope says technology can heal, connect, educate, and protect creation, but can also divide, exclude, and create injustice. Technology is “never neutral,” he writes, because it takes on the traits of those who devise, finance, regulate, and use it.
